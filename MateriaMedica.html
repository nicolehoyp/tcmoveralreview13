History & Development of Chinese Herbal Medicine中藥的歷史與發展

  • Formal development began in the Eastern Han dynasty (25–220 CE), although people had been using medicinal substances for many centuries before this.
  • Chinese medicine has produced a number of foundational classics.
  • 正式發展始於東漢時期（西元 25–220 年）；但在此之前，人們早已使用藥物達數個世紀之久。
  • 中醫學留下了多部奠基性的經典著作。

Huang Di Nei Jing 黃帝內經 — Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic

  • Written in the 1st century BC.
  • Compiles medical and lifestyle knowledge used for prolonging life.
  • Teaches "The Way" of living so as to reach the age of 100.
  • Presents a holistic way of life — diet, emotions, weather and environment, maintaining good health, etc.
  • Regarded as "the Bible" of TCM.

《黃帝內經》

  • 成書於公元前一世紀。
  • 彙整用以延年益壽的醫學與養生知識。
  • 闡述能盡享天年（活至百歲）的生活「大道」。
  • 提出整體性的生活觀——涵蓋飲食、情志、天時與環境、養護正氣等。
  • 被譽為中醫的「聖經」。

Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing 神農本草經 — Divine Husbandman's Classic of Materia Medica

  • The earliest pharmacopoeia; Shen Nong personally tested 365 herbs and recorded their function, grade, energy (temperature) and flavour, together with how to process and store them.

Shang Han Lun 傷寒論 — Discussion of Cold Damage

  • Written by Zhang Zhong Jing, c. 200 CE.
  • Focuses on herbal treatment of infectious illnesses and epidemic diseases.

《神農本草經》

  • 現存最早的藥典；神農親嚐三百六十五種藥物，記載其功效、品級、藥性（四氣）與氣味，以及炮製與貯藏之法。

《傷寒論》

  • 由張仲景撰於約公元 200 年。
  • 專論以方藥治療外感熱病與流行疫病。

Jin Gui Yao Lue 金匱要略 — Essentials from the Golden Cabinet

  • Also written by Zhang Zhong Jing.
  • The earliest monograph on the diagnosis and treatment of miscellaneous (internal) diseases.

Ben Cao Gang Mu 本草綱目 — Compendium of Materia Medica

  • Written in the Ming dynasty (1368–1644).
  • The most complete and comprehensive materia medica: lists ~1,800 herbs and ~11,000 formulas.

《金匱要略》

  • 亦為張仲景所著。
  • 現存最早論述雜病（內科病證）辨證論治的專著。

《本草綱目》

  • 成書於明代（1368–1644 年）。
  • 最完備、最全面的本草巨著：收載藥物約 1,800 種、方劑約 11,000 首。
02

Properties of Herbs — the Framework藥性總論（每味藥的描述架構）

Every herb is described through the same set of properties:

  • Nomenclature — Latin name and Pinyin name.
  • Temperature — where it sits on the Yin–Yang (cold–hot) balance.
  • Flavour — explains its function; ties to the Five Elements.
  • Channel — the location(s) where the action takes effect.
  • Action — a reflection of the temperature, flavour and channel combined.
  • Indication — the applied action: examples of signs and symptoms it can treat.
  • Dosage — governed by potency, toxicity, the botanical part used, and the patient's pre-existing conditions.

每一味藥都依同一組藥性項目來描述：

  • 命名——拉丁學名與拼音名。
  • 藥性（四氣／溫度）——在陰陽（寒—熱）天平上的位置。
  • 氣味（五味）——揭示其功用；與五行相應。
  • 歸經——藥力所作用的部位。
  • 功效——四氣、五味與歸經三者綜合的體現。
  • 主治（適應症）——功效的具體運用：所能治療的徵象與症狀舉例。
  • 劑量——取決於藥效強弱、毒性、入藥部位，以及患者的既往體質與病史。
03

Nomenclature命名法

Latin name

Built from up to three elements:

  • the botanical part used;
  • the species / harvest location;
  • the processing applied.

Pinyin name

Named after any of several logics:

  • Visual (colour, appearance) — e.g. Hong Hua (red flower).
  • Botanical part — e.g. Sang Ye (mulberry leaf).
  • Source / harvest location — e.g. Chuan Bei Mu (from Sichuan).
  • Literal meaning — character meaning or the herb's effect.
  • Some herbs also carry alternate names.

拉丁學名

最多由三部分組合而成：

  • 入藥的植物部位；
  • 物種／產地；
  • 所施加的炮製方法。

拼音藥名

命名邏輯有多種：

  • 外觀（顏色、形態）——如紅花。
  • 入藥部位——如桑葉。
  • 來源／產地——如川貝母（產於四川）。
  • 字面義——取字義或藥效命名。
  • 部分藥物另有別名。

Temperature (Four Qi)藥性 · 四氣（寒熱溫涼）

Hot > Warm > Slightly Warm > Neutral > Slightly Cool > Cool > Cold

  • Represents varying degrees of hot and cold.
  • Used to balance Yin and Yang in the body.

熱 > 溫 > 微溫 > 平 > 微涼 > 涼 > 寒

  • 代表寒熱程度的不同層級。
  • 用以調和人體的陰陽。
"Hot diseases need to be cooled; cold diseases need to be warmed."Nei Jing (Inner Classic), Chapter 74
「熱者寒之，寒者溫之。」《內經》第 74 篇（〈至真要大論〉旨義）
05

Flavours (Five Tastes)五味

There are five flavours, one for each of the elements:

  • Spicy / Acrid (Pungent)
  • Sweet
  • Bitter
  • Sour
  • Salty

Beyond the five:

  • Herbs with none of these flavours are called "bland".
  • "Aromatic" is a further flavour type meaning fragrant.
  • "Astringent" resembles sour/bitter carried to the point of contracting.
  • Herbs usually have more than one flavour.
  • An herb's taste/flavour determines its therapeutic properties.

五味與五行一一相應：

  • 辛（辣）
  • 甘（甜）
  • 苦
  • 酸
  • 鹹

五味之外：

  • 不具上述五味者，稱為「淡」。
  • 「芳香」為另一類氣味，指其芬芳走竄。
  • 「澀」近於酸／苦，且收斂至能「收攝」的程度。
  • 一味藥通常兼具多種氣味。
  • 藥物的氣味決定其治療特性。

Pungent / Acrid — Xin 辛 · Yang in nature (Lungs)

  • Usually reflects volatile-oil content; able to scatter Cold.
  • Induces perspiration — diaphoretic, expelling exogenous pathogens.
  • May cause Dryness if excessive.
  • Activating, pushing action — invigorates Blood and Qi.
  • Its dispersing action may lead to exhaustion of Yin and Qi.

Sweet — Gan 甘 · Yang in nature (Spleen)

  • Chiefly nourishes, tonifies, harmonizes, moistens and replenishes the body's Yin and Qi.
  • Alleviates pain and spasms; nourishes and tonifies the Middle Jiao.
  • Improves the taste of a formula.

辛（辣）· 性屬陽（歸肺）

  • 多因含揮發油；能散寒。
  • 發汗解表——祛除外感邪氣。
  • 過用則易致燥。
  • 具行散、推動之力——行氣活血。
  • 因其發散之性，久用或過用可耗傷陰氣。

甘（甜）· 性屬陽（歸脾）

  • 主於滋養、補益、調和、潤燥，充養人體之陰與氣。
  • 緩急止痛止痙；補養中焦。
  • 並能調和藥味，改善方劑口感。

Bitter — Ku 苦 · Yin in nature (Heart)

  • Drains Heat, dries Dampness, or descends the upward flow of Qi.
  • Can be cold or warm in nature to eliminate Dampness.
  • Slightly stimulates appetite and digestion.

Sour — Suan 酸 · Yin in nature (Liver)

  • Astringes excessive perspiration, spontaneous emission or leukorrhagia; astringes Qi, stops bleeding, relieves diarrhea.
  • Improves digestion and stimulates appetite.
  • Softens the arteries.

苦 · 性屬陰（歸心）

  • 能瀉熱、燥濕、降逆（降上逆之氣）。
  • 藥性可寒可溫，用以祛濕。
  • 略能開胃助消化。

酸 · 性屬陰（歸肝）

  • 收斂——固表止汗、澀精止帶；斂氣、止血、止瀉。
  • 助消化、開胃。
  • 柔潤脈道（軟化動脈）。

Salty — Xian 鹹 · Yin in nature (Kidneys)

  • Purges, drains, softens hard masses, reduces Phlegm.
  • E.g. constipation, scrofula, phlegm nodules.

Bland — Dan 淡 · Yang in nature

  • Seeps and drains; leaches out Dampness and promotes diuresis.
  • E.g. dysuria, edema.

Astringent — Se 澀 · Yin in nature

  • Similar to the sour drugs.
  • E.g. sweating from debility, diarrhea, bleeding, frequent urination, spermatorrhea, excessive perspiration.

鹹 · 性屬陰（歸腎）

  • 瀉下、軟堅散結、化痰。
  • 如：便秘、瘰癧、痰核。

淡 · 性屬陽

  • 滲濕利水；滲洩水濕、通利小便。
  • 如：小便不利、水腫。

澀 · 性屬陰

  • 作用近於酸味藥。
  • 如：虛汗、久瀉、出血、尿頻、遺精、多汗。

Channel Tropisms歸經

  • The location(s) where an herb is observed to exert its effect.
  • A location may be a region of the body, an organ, or a channel.
  • Most herbs act on more than one organ or channel.
  • An herb may also have the capacity to lead or guide the other herbs in a prescription to a particular location.
  • 指觀察到藥力所作用的部位。
  • 該部位可為身體某區域、某臟腑，或某經絡。
  • 多數藥物可同時歸入數經（作用於多個臟腑或經絡）。
  • 部分藥物尚具引經之能，引導方中他藥直達特定部位。

The channels (organs) herbs are assigned to

Twelve regular channels, grouped as organ pairs, plus the Triple Burner:

  • Heart · Small Intestine
  • Kidney · Bladder
  • Lung · Large Intestine
  • Spleen · Stomach
  • Liver · Gallbladder
  • Triple Burner (San Jiao) — Upper Burner / Middle Burner / Lower Burner

藥物所歸的經（臟腑）

十二正經，依臟腑表裡相配，另加三焦：

  • 心 · 小腸
  • 腎 · 膀胱
  • 肺 · 大腸
  • 脾 · 胃
  • 肝 · 膽
  • 三焦——上焦／中焦／下焦

Actions功效

  • An herb's action is linked to its temperature, flavour and channel.
    • E.g. a warm, acrid herb treats exterior Cold diseases: acrid disperses the pathogen while the warmth counters the Cold.
  • An herb can have one action on one channel/organ, yet a completely different action on another organ, depending on that organ's relationship to the flavour.
    • E.g. Gan Cao (licorice), being sweet, tonifies the Spleen — but it can also clear Heart Fire.
  • 藥物的功效，取決於其四氣、五味與歸經三者。
    • 如：溫而辛之藥治外感表寒證——辛以散邪，溫以祛寒。
  • 同一味藥於某經（臟）呈現一種作用，於另一臟卻可呈現迥異的作用，端視該臟與其氣味之關係而定。
    • 如：甘草味甘，能補脾；然亦能清心火。

The Eight Therapeutic Methods

From Yi Xue Xin Wu (Medical Revelations):

  • Promote sweating — exterior patterns.
  • Induce vomiting — acute GI diseases.
  • Purge — Heat; Damp-Heat.
  • Harmonize — disharmonies between organs.
  • Warm — Coldness; Yang.
  • Clear — Heat; Damp; Wind-Damp; Summer-Heat.
  • Tonify — deficiency patterns.
  • Reduce (sedate) — excess patterns.

八法（八種治法）

出自《醫學心悟》：

  • 汗法（發汗）——表證。
  • 吐法（催吐）——急性胃腸病證。
  • 下法（瀉下）——熱；濕熱。
  • 和法（和解）——臟腑不和。
  • 溫法（溫裡）——寒證；助陽。
  • 清法（清熱）——熱、濕、風濕、暑熱。
  • 補法（補益）——虛證。
  • 消法（消導／鎮攝）——實證。

Directional Actions升降浮沉（藥物趨向）

Herbs also act in particular directions.

Herbs that ascend / lift are used to:

  • Raise Yang
  • Lift Qi
  • Move upwards and outwards
  • Promote circulation
  • Promote sweating
  • Disperse Cold
  • Expel Wind

藥物亦具特定的作用趨向。

升／浮之藥用於：

  • 升陽
  • 舉氣（提舉中氣）
  • 向上、向外發散
  • 促進氣血運行
  • 發汗
  • 散寒
  • 祛風

Herbs that descend / lower are used to:

  • Move downwards and inwards
  • Redirect rebellious Qi downwards
  • Calm wheezing and coughing
  • Prevent leakage of fluids
  • Anchor rising Yang

降／沉之藥用於：

  • 向下、向內收斂
  • 降逆（引上逆之氣下行）
  • 平喘止咳
  • 固澀止漏（防津液外泄）
  • 潛陽（鎮潛上亢之陽）

Dosage劑量

By property → dosage level

  • Toxic, harsh, light, strong-flavoured herbs → lower.
  • Flowers, leaves, aromatic herbs → lower.
  • Moderate, bland, hard, heavy herbs → higher.
  • Roots, fruits, rich, heavy herbs → higher.
  • Minerals and shells → highest (over 30 g).
  • External applications → higher.
  • Fresh herbs → twice the amount of dried.

By usage

  • Single-herb prescription or main herb → higher.
  • Pill or powder form → lower.

依藥性定劑量

  • 有毒、峻烈、質輕、味厚之藥 → 宜小。
  • 花、葉、芳香之藥 → 宜小。
  • 性平、味淡、質硬、質重之藥 → 宜大。
  • 根、果、味厚、質重之藥 → 宜大。
  • 礦石、貝殼類 → 最大（可逾 30 克）。
  • 外用 → 宜大。
  • 鮮品 → 為乾品的兩倍。

依用法定劑量

  • 單味方或方中主藥 → 宜大。
  • 丸劑、散劑 → 宜小。

Standard reference points

  • Common dosage: 3–10 g (1–3 qian 錢).
  • Property of the drug: drastic/toxic drugs start small and increase gradually; low-potency or dense drugs (minerals, shells) are given in larger doses.
  • Patient: strong vs weak constitution (especially Stomach-Qi strength); elderly; weight; age (see charts).
  • Pattern of disease: acute → higher, chronic → lower; serious → higher, mild → lower.
  • Others: potency & quality (wild vs cultivated); environmental/seasonal factors (e.g. more Damp-draining herbs in hot humid weather); form (decoction higher, pill/powder lower); the drug's role in the formula.

常用劑量參照

  • 常用量：3–10 克（1–3 錢）。
  • 藥物之性：峻烈、有毒者宜自小量始、漸增；藥效弱或質重者（礦石、貝殼）宜大量。
  • 患者因素：體質強弱（尤重胃氣盛衰）；老年；體重；年齡（見下表）。
  • 病證性質：急者宜大、緩者宜小；重者宜大、輕者宜小。
  • 其他：藥材品質（野生 vs 栽培）；環境／季節（如炎熱潮濕時祛濕藥宜加量）；劑型（湯劑量大，丸散量小）；藥物在方中的地位與作用。

Processing of Herbs (Pao Zhi)中藥炮製

Chinese medicinals are made of plant, animal or mineral material and should be processed before being taken as drugs. Purposes of processing:

  • Increase potency / effectiveness — e.g. Ban Xia descends rebellious Qi and stops vomiting; processed with ginger (Sheng Jiang) it is even more effective.
  • Reduce potency / minimize side effects or toxicity — e.g. aconitine is the toxic component of Chuan Wu (often causing arrhythmia); boiling/steaming Chuan Wu with Gan Cao and black beans lowers aconitine and thus toxicity.
  • Change the action or site of action — e.g. Da Huang usually promotes bowel movements, but processed Da Huang can instead clear Heat and detoxify.
  • Accentuate a specific action — e.g. charring helps herbs stop bleeding better.
  • Ease preparation or storage — e.g. shell/mineral drugs are ground to powder to dissolve more easily; plant herbs are dried for better storage.
  • Clear unwanted substances — grit, unpleasant odors, pests, impurities.

中藥取材於植物、動物或礦物，入藥前多須炮製。炮製之目的：

  • 增強藥效——如半夏能降逆止嘔，以生薑製之則效力更著。
  • 減緩藥性、消減毒副作用——如烏頭鹼為川烏之毒性成分（常致心律不整）；以甘草、黑豆同煮或蒸川烏，可降低烏頭鹼含量、減毒。
  • 改變作用或作用部位——如大黃生用瀉下通便，製後（熟大黃）則轉為清熱解毒。
  • 突出某一特定功效——如炒炭能增強止血之力。
  • 便於製備或貯藏——如貝殼、礦石類研末以利溶出；植物藥乾燥以利久貯。
  • 去除雜質——砂石、異味、蟲蛀、雜質等。

Physical processing

  • Used for cleaning and picking out impurities.
  • E.g. sorting, sifting, peeling, winnowing, scraping, brushing.

Liquid processing

  • Moistening — soften with a small amount of liquid (water, wine, ginger juice, etc.) to make cutting easier.
  • Rinsing — place in running or frequently-changed water to remove dirt, odors, salt or toxic components.
  • Aqueous trituration (water-grinding) — for mineral drugs: ground with water into fine powder that stays suspended; the suspension is settled and the precipitate dried into a fine powder for use.

物理（修治）處理

  • 用於淨選、去除雜質。
  • 如：挑揀、篩選、去皮、簸揚、刮削、刷洗。

水製處理

  • 潤——以少量液體（水、酒、薑汁等）潤軟，便於切製。
  • 漂洗——置流水或屢換之水中，去污垢、異味、鹽分或毒性成分。
  • 水飛——多用於礦物藥：與水共研成極細粉懸浮於水中，取上懸液沉澱，乾燥後得極細藥粉備用。

Fire processing

  • Dry-frying (Chao 炒) — dries the herb for storage; increases Spleen-awakening properties.
  • Quick / blast frying (Pao 炮) — fried at very high heat until dark brown; reduces toxicity or moderates harsh properties.
  • Charring / carbonizing (Tan 炭) — enhances the ability to stop bleeding.
  • Roasting in hot ashes (Wei 煨) — wrap the drug in wet paper, paste or mud and set in hot ashes until the outer layer chars; the drug itself is not charred and its toxicity/intensity is reduced.

火製處理

  • 炒——乾燥藥材以利貯藏；增強醒脾之性。
  • 炮——猛火急炒至深褐色；減毒或緩其峻烈之性。
  • 炒炭（炭）——增強止血之效。
  • 煨——以濕紙、麵糊或濕泥裹藥，埋入熱灰中至外層焦黑；藥體本身不焦，其毒性或烈性得以減緩。

Fire processing — frying with liquid (Zhi 炙)

Baked while mixed with a fluid to boost therapeutic effect or reduce adverse effects:

  • Honey (Mi 蜜) — enhances moistening and tonifying.
  • Vinegar (Cu 醋) — enhances astringent, analgesic, blood-invigorating, detoxifying actions.
  • Wine (Jiu 酒) — enhances clearing channel blockage, expelling Wind, alleviating pain.
  • Ginger juice (Jiang 薑) — reduces the tendency of bitter-cold herbs to upset the stomach.
  • Salt (Yan 鹽) — guides the herb to Kidney / Lower Jiao.

Calcining (Duan 煅)

  • Strong fire applied directly until the drug turns red; makes it brittle and easy to pulverize. Usually for mineral and shell drugs.

火製 · 加輔料炙製（炙）

與液體輔料同炒烘，以增效或減不良反應：

  • 蜜炙——增強潤燥、補益。
  • 醋炙——增強收斂、止痛、活血、解毒。
  • 酒炙——增強通經活絡、祛風、止痛。
  • 薑汁炙——減緩苦寒藥傷胃之弊。
  • 鹽炙——引藥入腎／下焦。

煅

  • 以猛火直接煅燒至紅透，使藥質脆而易研末；多用於礦石、貝殼類藥物。

Processing by both water and fire

  • Boiling (Zhu 煮) — boiled in water or another liquid (vinegar, wine) to reduce side effects or increase potency.
  • Steaming (Zheng 蒸) — steamed in water or wine vapour to attenuate side effects or increase potency.
  • Quenching (Cui 淬) — for mineral/shell drugs: made red-hot then plunged into water or vinegar/wine to make it light and crisp, easy to powder, and to moderate its properties.
  • Simmering (Ao 熬) — boiled through several changes of water and condensed into a thickened liquid, syrup or gel.

水火共製

  • 煮——以水或他液（醋、酒）煮之，減副作用或增藥效。
  • 蒸——以水蒸氣或酒蒸，減副作用或增藥效。
  • 淬——用於礦石／貝殼藥：燒紅後投入水或醋、酒中，使之輕脆、易研末，並緩和其藥性。
  • 熬——屢換水久煮，濃縮成稠汁、膏滋或膠凍。

Methods of Administration (Dosage Forms)給藥方法（劑型）

The main dosage forms:

  • Decoction / soups
  • Drafts (short-decocted powders)
  • Powder
  • Pill
  • Syrups
  • Plasters
  • Medicinal wines / tinctures

主要劑型：

  • 湯劑（煎湯）
  • 煮散（藥散短煎）
  • 散劑（粉末）
  • 丸劑
  • 膏滋／糖漿
  • 膏藥
  • 藥酒／酊劑

Decoction / soups — the conventional preparation

  • Use an earthenware or enamel pot — NOT metal. Cover the herbs with clean water.
  • Soak 40–60 min, bring to a boil on high heat, then simmer 20–40 min until about 1 cup of liquid remains.
  • Strain; add fresh water and boil the herbs again (this may be done a third time).
  • Combine the liquid from the 2–3 batches and divide into 2 portions taken across the day — this is one day's dose.
  • If a formula is overcooked or burned, do not add water and cook it again.

湯劑——傳統煎法

  • 用陶器或搪瓷鍋，忌用金屬鍋。加清水沒過藥面。
  • 浸泡 40–60 分鐘，大火煮沸後轉小火煎 20–40 分鐘，至餘約 1 杯藥液。
  • 濾出藥液；再加清水復煎（可煎至第三次）。
  • 將 2–3 次藥液混合，分作兩份於一日內服完——此為一日之劑量。
  • 若煎糊或煎焦，切勿再加水重煎。

Special decoction handling

  • Decoct first — for toxic herbs (30–45 min before); firm/hard herbs (minerals, shells) or those whose actives are hard to boil out (10–20 min before); lightweight herbs needing a large, bulky dose (20 min before).
  • Decoct last — aromatic herbs, or those whose actives evaporate/decompose easily; add in the last 15 (or even 5) minutes. Some end-added herbs that are too strong (e.g. Da Huang) may be decocted with the rest to mute their effect.
  • Wrap before decoction — powdered herbs, minerals, small seeds, or herbs with cilia that irritate the throat/GI tract; wrap in cheesecloth or gauze.
  • Decoct separately — certain valuable herbs given in small amounts (e.g. Ren Shen); also toxic herbs.
  • Add to the strained decoction — highly viscous/sticky herbs that would stick and burn; also some easily-dissolving powders.
  • Take with the decoction — expensive aromatics ground to powder, taken first then chased with the decoction.

特殊煎服法

  • 先煎——有毒藥（先煎 30–45 分鐘）；質堅硬者（礦石、貝殼）或有效成分難以煎出者（先煎 10–20 分鐘）；質輕但用量大、佔鍋者（先煎 20 分鐘）。
  • 後下——芳香藥，或有效成分易揮發／分解者；於最後 15 分鐘（甚至 5 分鐘）下鍋。有些本應後下但藥性過猛者（如大黃），可與他藥同煎以緩其效。
  • 包煎——粉末藥、礦石、細小種子，或帶絨毛易刺激咽喉／消化道者，以紗布或粗棉布包裹。
  • 另煎——貴重且用量少之藥（如人參），或有毒藥物。
  • 烊化／沖入——極黏稠者不宜同煎（易黏鍋燒焦），入濾出藥液中；亦適用於易溶之粉末。
  • 沖服（隨湯送服）——貴重芳香藥研末，先服再以湯藥送下。

Powder

  • Readily absorbable; convenient; easily stored and long-lasting.

Drafts

  • Powders decocted for ~10 min; dosage much smaller than decoctions.

Pill

  • Fine powder combined with a viscous medium — water, honey, or even wax.
  • Ranges in size from a mustard seed to a longan fruit.
  • Mild and slow in action.

散劑（粉末）

  • 易吸收、服用便利、易貯久存。

煮散

  • 將藥散煎約 10 分鐘；用量遠小於湯劑。

丸劑

  • 以細粉與黏性賦形劑（水、蜜，甚或蠟）製成。
  • 大小自芥子至龍眼不等。
  • 藥性和緩、作用徐緩。

Syrups

  • Substances decocted in water, the decoction reduced, then thickened with sugar or honey.
  • Used for chronic debilitating diseases, cough, or sore throat.

Plasters

  • Herbs powdered — or the decoction mixed with oil (usually sesame) with beeswax added — spread on paper or cloth, hardened, and applied to the skin.
  • Used externally, usually for dermatological conditions; also abscesses, boils, arthritis/pain, fractures and sprains, fixed masses.

Medicinal wines / tinctures

  • Herbs steeped in wine (usually rice wine) for days, weeks or months.
  • Wine is considered nourishing, blood-invigorating and channel-unblocking.
  • Used for arthritis, trauma/injury, and deficiency conditions.

膏滋／糖漿

  • 諸藥加水煎煮，濃縮藥汁，再以糖或蜜收膏。
  • 用於慢性虛損性疾病、咳嗽或咽喉腫痛。

膏藥

  • 將藥研末，或以煎汁與油（多用麻油）調合、加蜂蠟，攤於紙或布上，待其凝固後貼於皮膚。
  • 屬外用，多用於皮膚病；亦用於癰腫瘡癤、關節炎／疼痛、骨折扭傷、癥瘕積聚。

藥酒／酊劑

  • 將藥浸於酒（多用米酒）中數日、數週或數月。
  • 酒被視為能滋養、活血、通經。
  • 用於關節炎、跌打損傷及虛損諸證。

How & when to take a decoction

  • Generally taken warm.
  • Acute cases: 2 (up to 3) doses daily, or every 4 hours.
  • Chronic cases: 1 dose daily or every other day.
  • Anti-emetic formulas: take frequently in small amounts.
  • Unconscious patients or trismus (lockjaw): may be fed through the nose.
  • Diaphoretic / pathogen-expelling formulas: take warm to promote sweating.
  • Purgatives: take until diarrhea or vomiting subsides.
  • Tonifying herbs/formulas: take before meals.
  • Anti-parasitic & purging herbs: take on an empty stomach.
  • Mind-calming formulas: take before sleep.
  • Herbs that irritate the GI tract (or if the patient has GI adverse effects): take after meals.

湯劑的服法與服藥時機

  • 一般宜溫服。
  • 急證：每日 2 劑（至多 3 劑），或每 4 小時一次。
  • 慢證：每日 1 劑或隔日 1 劑。
  • 止嘔方：宜少量頻服。
  • 神昏或牙關緊閉者：可經鼻飼給藥。
  • 發汗解表／祛外邪方：宜溫服以助發汗。
  • 瀉下藥：服至瀉止或嘔止即停。
  • 補益藥／方：宜飯前服。
  • 驅蟲與瀉下藥：宜空腹服。
  • 安神方：宜睡前服。
  • 易刺激胃腸之藥（或患者有胃腸不適者）：宜飯後服。

Herb Combinations配伍

1 · Mutual Accentuation (Xiang Xu 相須)

  • Two substances with similar functions combined to accentuate their therapeutic actions.
  • E.g. Shi Gao + Zhi Mu to clear Heat and drain Fire; Da Huang + Mang Xiao to purge the intestines.

2 · Mutual Enhancement (Xiang Shi 相使)

  • Two or more substances of different actions where one enhances the effect of the other in a specific situation.
  • E.g. Fu Ling + Huang Qi for edema; Da Huang + Huang Qi for painful, red eyes.

一 · 相須

  • 兩味功用相似之藥合用，以增強療效。
  • 如：石膏＋知母清熱瀉火；大黃＋芒硝瀉下通腸。

二 · 相使

  • 兩味以上功用不同之藥合用，一藥能於特定情形下增強他藥之效。
  • 如：茯苓＋黃耆治水腫；大黃＋黃耆治目赤腫痛。

3 · Mutual Counteraction (Xiang Wei 相畏)

  • One substance's toxicity or side effects are reduced or eliminated by another.
  • E.g. Zhi Ban Xia + Sheng Jiang — Sheng Jiang reduces the toxicity of Ban Xia.

4 · Mutual Suppression (Xiang Sha 相殺)

  • The converse of counteraction — one substance reduces the undesirable side effects of another.
  • E.g. Sheng Jiang is used to kill/suppress the toxicity of Zhi Ban Xia.

Note: Counteraction (相畏) and Suppression (相殺) describe the same pairing from the two herbs' opposite viewpoints.

三 · 相畏

  • 一藥之毒性或副作用，能被他藥所減輕或消除。
  • 如：製半夏＋生薑——生薑能減半夏之毒。

四 · 相殺

  • 與相畏互為表裡——一藥能減除他藥之不良副作用。
  • 如：生薑能殺（制）製半夏之毒。

按：相畏與相殺乃就同一組配伍、自兩藥相反的角度而言。

Paired herbs — Dui Yao 對藥

Famous paired combinations, which tend to:

  • Enhance complementary actions
  • Treat both root and branch
  • Treat two divisions at once — e.g. Qi and Blood
  • Treat two different pathologies at once
  • Harmonize interior and exterior
  • Tonify and drain at the same time
  • Reinforce an action
  • Upbear and downbear at the same time
  • Protect · Harmonize · Guide

對藥（配對藥）

著名的成對配伍，多具下列作用：

  • 增強互補功效
  • 標本兼治
  • 兩部同治——如氣與血同調
  • 同治兩種不同病機
  • 調和表裡
  • 補瀉並行
  • 加強某一功效
  • 升降並用
  • 護（護正）· 和（調和）· 引（引經）

Formula Structure方劑結構（君臣佐使）

Four roles of herbs within a formula:

  • 1 · Chief (Jun 君) — the principal drug; provides the principal curative action; exerts the major, direct effect on the main condition or addresses the most important treatment principle; usually given at a higher dose than the rest.
  • 2 · Deputy (Chen 臣) — addresses secondary treatment strategies, or strengthens the action of the Chief.
  • 3 · Assistant (Zuo 佐) — supports Chief and Deputy; reinforces actions; reduces toxicity; reduces side effects.
  • 4 · Envoy (Shi 使) — guides the formula to a body part, channel or organ; harmonizes the formula.

方中藥物的四種地位：

  • 一 · 君藥——方中主藥；主司主要治療作用；針對主病主證發揮直接的主要功效，或體現最重要的治則；用量通常較他藥為大。
  • 二 · 臣藥——針對兼證的次要治法，或加強君藥之力。
  • 三 · 佐藥——輔助君、臣；加強療效；減低毒性；消減副作用。
  • 四 · 使藥——引方藥直達病所（部位、經絡或臟腑）；調和諸藥。

Herb toxicity — common causes

  • Incorrect dosage (too high)
  • Incorrect processing (toxicity not removed)
  • Incorrect combination (incl. herb–drug interaction)
  • Incorrect diagnosis
  • Incorrect constitution (wrong for the patient)
  • Incorrect administration — improper cooking, overdose, not taken within the effective window

中藥中毒——常見成因

  • 劑量不當（過大）
  • 炮製不當（未除毒性）
  • 配伍不當（含中藥—西藥相互作用）
  • 辨證／診斷有誤

Safe-Practice Terms & Adverse Events安全用藥術語與不良事件

Key terms

  • Side-effect — an undesirable effect alongside the desired one; often predictable or common.
  • Intolerance — a lowered threshold before side effects appear; tolerance varies, so dosage must be adjusted.
  • Adverse Event (AE) — an unexpected, unforeseen undesirable effect of treatment.
  • Allergic reaction — unpredictable immune over-reaction to a normally harmless substance; stay vigilant.
  • Contraindication — a condition in which a treatment must not be done: Absolute (risk > benefit, never do it), Relative (modify or add vigilance), Local (redirect to another area/concern).

重要術語

  • 副作用——伴隨療效出現的非預期不良反應；多可預測、常見。
  • 不耐受——出現副作用的閾值降低；耐受度因人而異，須調整劑量。
  • 不良事件（AE）——治療中非預期、未能預見的不良效應。
  • 過敏反應——免疫系統對常屬無害之物的過度反應，難以預測，須警覺。
  • 禁忌——不應施行之情境：絕對禁忌（風險大於獲益，絕不可行）、相對禁忌（須調整或加強監測）、局部禁忌（改治他處或他證）。

Handling adverse events

  • Minor AEs — inform patients of possible reactions; welcome their feedback to guide adjustments; recheck dose, frequency and whether the medicine is taken correctly.
  • Serious AEs — rare; refer to poison control, family physician or the ER as needed. In Canada, report via the Canada Vigilance Adverse Reaction form. Reporting ensures patient follow-up, builds safety data, and can reflect professionalism in any malpractice complaint.

不良事件之處置

  • 輕微 AE——事先告知患者可能反應；傾聽其描述以調整處方；覆核劑量、頻次及是否正確服藥。
  • 嚴重 AE——罕見；視需要轉介毒物中心、家庭醫師或急診。在加拿大以 Canada Vigilance 不良反應表通報。通報可確保追蹤、累積安全數據，並在醫療糾紛中彰顯專業。

General Contraindications & Toxicity Categories一般禁忌與毒性分級

General contraindications & precautions

  • Polypharmacy (use of more than 5 medications).
  • Abnormal liver tests after starting treatment.
  • Existing liver or kidney disease.
  • Previous allergic reactions.
  • First trimester of pregnancy.
  • Children in their first year of life.

一般禁忌與注意

  • 多重用藥（同時使用逾 5 種藥物）。
  • 治療後肝功能檢查異常。
  • 已有肝或腎疾病。
  • 既往有過敏反應史。
  • 妊娠第一孕期。
  • 一歲以內嬰兒。

Three traditional toxicity grades

  • Heavy toxicity — may cause coma, nausea, numbness of mouth and limbs: Ba Dou; Fu Zi / Cao Wu / Chuan Wu / Wu Tou (aconites); Ban Xia; Zhu Sha (cinnabar); Tian Nan Xing; Cang Er Zi; Guang Fang Ji.
  • Minor toxicity — dizziness, abnormal heartbeat or mild breathing difficulty at high doses: Xing Ren; Bai Guo; Tian Hua Fen; Ma Huang; Xi Xin.
  • No toxicity — commonly used medicinals with few side effects.

傳統三級毒性

  • 大毒——可致昏迷、噁心、口與四肢麻木：巴豆；附子／草烏／川烏／烏頭；半夏；硃砂；天南星；蒼耳子；廣防己。
  • 小毒——高劑量時頭暈、心律異常或輕度呼吸困難：杏仁；白果；天花粉；麻黃；細辛。
  • 無毒——常用、副作用少之藥。

Reducing toxicity

  • TCM manages toxicity by combining, processing and decoction; most herbs are safe with correct pattern differentiation, dose and duration.
  • Xiang Wei (counteraction) / Xiang Sha (suppression): Sheng Jiang moderates Ban Xia; Gan Cao moderates Fu Zi; Bing Lang moderates the nausea from Chang Shan.
  • Decoct first 30–60 min: Chuan Wu, Fu Zi, Cao Wu — prolonged decoction greatly reduces toxicity; add other herbs afterwards.
  • Modern texts add pharmacologic/clinical research and usually an LD50 (dose lethal to 50% of test animals). See also the Hong Kong Chinese Medicine Council Schedule 1 toxic herbs list.

減毒之法

  • 中醫以配伍、炮製、煎煮制毒；多數藥物在辨證正確、劑量與療程得宜下皆屬安全。
  • 相畏／相殺：生薑制半夏；甘草制附子；檳榔制常山之噁心。
  • 先煎 30–60 分鐘：川烏、附子、草烏——久煎大幅減毒，之後再下他藥。
  • 現代典籍增列藥理／臨床研究，多附 LD50（使半數試驗動物致死之劑量）。另可參香港中醫藥管理委員會附表一毒劇中藥。

Hepatotoxicity (modern caution)

  • A 2015 systematic review (Teschke, Zhang et al., Annals of Hepatology) documented 28 Chinese herbs/formulas with hepatotoxic effects — including Bo He, Chuan Lian Zi, Da Huang, Gan Cao, Ge Gen, He Shou Wu, Huang Qin, Ren Shen and Ma Huang.
  • Idiosyncratic Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI) depends on host factors (age, gender, pregnancy, obesity, comorbid liver disease), drug factors (daily dose, interactions, polypharmacy) and environment (alcohol, smoking).
  • Ephedra (Ma Huang) has been linked to heart attack and stroke; the FDA banned ephedra dietary supplements in 2004 (the ban does not extend to TCM remedies). US TCM herbs are sold as dietary supplements under looser FDA rules — watch for contamination with drugs, toxins or heavy metals.

肝毒性（現代警示）

  • 2015 年一篇系統性回顧（Teschke、Zhang 等，《Annals of Hepatology》）記載 28 種具肝毒性之中藥／方——含薄荷、川楝子、大黃、甘草、葛根、何首烏、黃芩、人參、麻黃等。
  • 特異體質性藥物性肝損傷（DILI）取決於宿主（年齡、性別、妊娠、肥胖、原有肝病）、藥物（每日劑量、交互作用、多重用藥）與環境（飲酒、吸菸）。
  • 麻黃與心臟病、中風相關；FDA 於 2004 年禁售含麻黃膳食補充劑（不含 TCM 用藥）。美國 TCM 藥多以膳食補充劑販售、規範較鬆，須防藥物、毒素或重金屬污染。

Interactions — Herb, Drug & Diet交互作用 · 藥—藥／藥—西藥／藥—食

Herb–herb

  • Since the Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing, tradition settled on 19 antagonisms (十九畏) and 18 incompatibilities (十八反) — combinations that reduce each other’s effect or cause toxicity (see the mnemonic map in §14).
  • These are not absolute; many modern practitioners set them aside, and research has shown some to be incorrect (Bensky & Gamble).

Herb–diet

  • Ren Shen — avoid turnip / radish.
  • Sheng/Shu Di Huang & He Shou Wu — avoid onion, garlic, radish.
  • Fu Ling — avoid vinegar.
  • Alcohol — a CNS depressant, liver-metabolized. Grapefruit — inhibits CYP3A4, altering levels of drugs on that pathway (calcium-channel blockers, statins, immunosuppressants, benzodiazepines, SSRIs).

藥—藥

  • 自《神農本草經》以降，漸定為十九畏與十八反——合用可削弱藥效或生毒（詳見 §14 口訣圖）。
  • 此非絕對；現代多有不拘者，研究亦證部分有誤（Bensky & Gamble）。

藥—食

  • 人參——忌蘿蔔。
  • 生／熟地黃、何首烏——忌蔥、蒜、蘿蔔。
  • 茯苓——忌醋。
  • 酒——中樞抑制、經肝代謝。葡萄柚——抑制 CYP3A4，改變經此途徑代謝藥物之濃度（鈣通道阻斷劑、他汀、免疫抑制劑、苯二氮平、SSRI）。

Herb–drug (highest-risk classes)

  • Sympathomimetic — may interfere with anti-hypertensives and anti-seizure drugs. e.g. Ma Huang; caution in hypertension, seizures, diabetes, thyroid disease.
  • Anticoagulant / antiplatelet — blood-invigorating herbs (Dan Shen, Dang Gui, Chuan Xiong, Tao Ren, Hong Hua, Shui Zhi) with warfarin, heparin, Plavix, aspirin → risk of excessive bleeding, even hemorrhagic stroke.
  • Diuretic — Fu Ling, Zhu Ling, Che Qian Cao, Ze Xie with diuretic drugs → harder BP control, hypotensive episodes, fainting.
  • Antidiabetic — herbs (Zhi Mu + Shi Gao; Xuan Shen + Cang Zhu; Shan Yao; Huang Qi) may further lower glucose → monitor for hyper/hypo-glycemic swings.

藥—西藥（高風險類別）

  • 擬交感——或干擾降壓與抗癲癇藥。如麻黃；高血壓、癲癇、糖尿病、甲狀腺病者慎用。
  • 抗凝／抗血小板——活血化瘀藥（丹參、當歸、川芎、桃仁、紅花、水蛭）與 warfarin、heparin、Plavix、aspirin 併用 → 出血過度，甚至出血性中風。
  • 利尿——茯苓、豬苓、車前草、澤瀉與利尿劑併用 → 血壓更難控制、低血壓、暈厥。
  • 降糖——知母＋石膏、玄參＋蒼朮、山藥、黃耆等或進一步降糖 → 須監測血糖起伏。

妊娠——相對禁忌

  • 桃仁 · 紅花 · 附子 · 乾薑 · 肉桂 · 枳實

Lactation

  • Assume all herbs pass into breast milk; advise lactating parents to avoid herbs.
  • Lipophilic materials (animal/insect substances, seeds) are more likely to appear in milk; hydrophilic materials (minerals, leaves, roots) less so.
  • The herb’s action on the parent will likely act on the baby too.
  • Promote lactation: Chuan Mu Tong, Chuan Shan Jia (pangolin!), Dong Kui Zi, Lou Lu, Nan Gua Zi, Si Gua Luo, Tong Cao, Wang Bu Liu Xing, Zi He Che.
  • Inhibit lactation: Mang Xiao, Shen Qu, Mai Ya, Hua Jiao.

哺乳

  • 假定所有藥物皆會進入乳汁；宜勸哺乳者避免用藥。
  • 親脂性藥（動物／昆蟲藥、種子）較易入乳；親水性藥（礦物、葉、根）較不易。
  • 藥對母體之作用，多半也會作用於嬰兒。
  • 下乳（促乳）：川木通、穿山甲（記得穿山甲！）、冬葵子、漏蘆、南瓜子、絲瓜絡、通草、王不留行、紫河車。
  • 回乳（抑乳）：芒硝、神麴、麥芽、花椒。

Herbal Pharmacy — Quality, Storage & Dispensing中藥房 · 品質、貯藏、處方與調劑

Raw herbs & storage

  • Ensure quality: species authenticity, hygienic manufacture, clear batch identification, no microbial contamination, and control of heavy metals, pesticides, aflatoxins, residual solvents and sulfur fumigation.
  • Store in clean, dry containers protected from insects and rodents; discard herbs showing deterioration, mold, discoloration or infestation.
  • Keep away from children — especially toxic herbs. Some herbs need air-tight containers, some airflow, some refrigeration, some protection from light.

生藥與貯藏

  • 確保品質：品種真偽、衛生製造、批號清楚、無微生物污染，並管控重金屬、農藥、黃麴毒素、殘留溶劑與硫燻。
  • 以潔淨乾燥容器貯存，防蟲防鼠；凡變質、發霉、變色或蟲蛀者棄之。
  • 遠離孩童——尤其毒藥。部分藥宜密封、部分需通氣、部分需冷藏、部分需避光。

Prescription writing

A prescription should include:

  • Practitioner’s name, address, phone; patient’s name; date.
  • Each herb’s name (and part, if relevant); method of preparation (whole, powder, granule, tincture) and processing.
  • Quantity of each herb; preparation instructions; number of packets/dosage; how to take.

Latin names matter

  • Latinate names avoid confusion — e.g. Da Ji = Herba sive Radix Cirsii Japonici (regulate Blood) vs Da Ji = Radix Euphorbiae Pekinensis (harsh cathartic). Chinese/Pinyin may be added.
  • Granules & manufactured medicines: specify weights, total weight, formula name, manufacturer, batch number, dosage and directions.

處方書寫

處方應載明：

  • 醫師姓名、地址、電話；患者姓名；日期。
  • 各藥名（必要時註明部位）；製備方法（全、粉、顆粒、酊劑）與炮製。
  • 各藥用量；煎製說明；帖數／劑量；服法。

拉丁名之必要

  • 拉丁名可免混淆——如大戟＝理血藥之 Herba sive Radix Cirsii Japonici（大薊）vs 峻下藥之 Radix Euphorbiae Pekinensis（京大戟）。可另附中文或拼音。
  • 顆粒與成藥：須註明各藥重量、總重、方名、製造商、批號、劑量與用法。

CITES & WAPPRIITA華盛頓公約與 WAPPRIITA

CITES

  • Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species, formed 1975 — ensures international trade does not threaten the survival of wild animals and plants.
  • Covers over 5,000 animal and 29,000 plant species; import/export of listed species is restricted and requires permits — including live specimens, parts, derivatives and by-products (herbs and medicines). Non-threatened look-alikes may also be restricted.

The three appendices

  • Appendix I — threatened with extinction. Both export and import permits required; commercial trade prohibited.
  • Appendix II — not yet threatened but could become so without regulated trade. Export permit (or re-export certificate) required.
  • Appendix III — under special management in certain countries. Export permit or certificate of origin required.

CITES 華盛頓公約

  • 《瀕危野生動植物種國際貿易公約》，成立於 1975 年——確保國際貿易不威脅野生動植物之存續。
  • 涵蓋逾 5,000 種動物與 29,000 種植物；受列物種之進出口受限、須許可證——含活體、部位、衍生物與副產品（藥材與成藥）。與瀕危種相似之非瀕危種亦可能受限。

三個附錄

  • 附錄一——瀕臨滅絕。進、出口許可證俱需，禁止商業貿易。
  • 附錄二——尚未瀕危，但若不規範貿易恐將瀕危。須出口許可證（或再出口證明）。
  • 附錄三——特定國家內特別管理之物種。須出口許可證或原產地證明。

WAPPRIITA (Canada)

  • Wild Animal and Plant Protection and Regulation of International and Interprovincial Trade Act — CITES is administered by Environment Canada and implemented under WAPPRIITA.
  • Under WAPPRIITA it is unlawful to: import or possess any wild animal/plant (or parts/derivatives) obtained or exported in violation of a foreign law (not limited to CITES species); import/export CITES-listed species without permits; or offer for sale or possess Appendix I species.

WAPPRIITA（加拿大）

  • 《野生動植物保護暨國際及省際貿易管理法》——CITES 由加拿大環境部主管，並依 WAPPRIITA 施行。
  • 依 WAPPRIITA，下列為違法：進口或持有任何違反外國法令而取得或出口之野生動植物（或其部位／衍生物，不限 CITES 物種）；未經許可進出口 CITES 物種；販售或持有附錄一物種。

Restricted Species & Population Status受限物種與現況

Commonly restricted medicinals

  • Chuan Shan Jia (pangolin scale) · She Xiang (musk) · Gui Ban (turtle plastron) · Hai Ma (seahorse) · Mu Xiang (aucklandia) · Chen Xiang (aquilaria) · Shi Hu (dendrobium) · Tian Ma (gastrodia).

Population status

  • Rhino — down >90% in 40 years. West African & Northern White: extinct in the wild; Javan ~60; Sumatran <80; Southern White ~18,000.
  • Tiger — Siberian recovered from ~40 (1940s) to ~500 after anti-poaching law; 6 other subspecies ~3,500 (from ~100,000 c.1900).
  • Saiga antelope — horn demand wiped out the Chinese population; ~30,000 remain in Russia & Kazakhstan.
  • Seahorses & pangolins — declining from habitat loss, souvenirs and medicine; trade is hard to track when taken as pills/capsules.

常見受限藥材

  • 穿山甲（鱗）· 麝香 · 龜板 · 海馬 · 木香 · 沉香 · 石斛 · 天麻。

族群現況

  • 犀牛——40 年內銳減逾九成。西非與北方白犀：野外滅絕；爪哇犀約 60 頭；蘇門答臘犀 <80；南方白犀約 18,000。
  • 虎——西伯利亞虎自 1940 年代約 40 頭，經反盜獵法回升至約 500；其餘六亞種約 3,500（1900 年前後曾約 10 萬）。
  • 賽加羚羊——對羚羊角之需求已使中國族群滅絕；俄羅斯與哈薩克尚存約 30,000。
  • 海馬與穿山甲——因棲地喪失、紀念品與藥用而減少；製成丸／膠囊後貿易難以追查。

Conservation measures

  • Successfully farmed herbs: Mu Xiang, Tian Ma, Shi Hu, Rou Cong Rong, etc.
  • Farmed animals: turtles, cattle, Cordyceps, etc.
  • National and international education and policing.

保育措施

  • 成功栽培之藥材：木香、天麻、石斛、肉蓯蓉等。
  • 養殖動物：龜、牛、蟲草等。
  • 國內與國際之教育與執法。
